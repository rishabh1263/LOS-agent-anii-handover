# LOS glossary

Each entry: the term, its full form, what it means, and an example. These are general meanings used across Indian
lenders. A company's own limits, rates, fees and timelines are not stated here; they come from its product policy.
Entries marked **Owner review** are correct as general knowledge but depend on a rule that can change.

## Loan
Loan: Money lent by a lender to a borrower, to be repaid with interest over an agreed period.
Example: a home loan of ₹30,00,000 repaid over 20 years.

## Principal
Principal: The amount actually borrowed, excluding interest. Each EMI repays part of the principal and the interest on it.
Example: on a ₹5,00,000 loan the principal is ₹5,00,000.

## Interest
Interest: The cost of borrowing, charged on the outstanding principal at the agreed rate.
Example: at 12% a year, ₹1,00,000 outstanding costs about ₹1,000 interest for a month.

## ROI (Rate of Interest)
ROI (Rate of Interest): The yearly percentage a lender charges on the outstanding loan. It may be fixed or floating.
Example: "ROI 10.5% p.a." means 10.5% a year on the outstanding principal.

## Fixed rate
Fixed rate: An interest rate that does not change for the agreed period, so the EMI stays the same.
Example: a personal loan at a fixed 13% for 3 years.

## Floating rate
Floating rate: An interest rate linked to a benchmark (such as the repo rate or MCLR) that moves when the benchmark changes; the EMI
or the tenure changes with it.
Example: a home loan priced at repo rate plus a spread.

## EMI (Equated Monthly Instalment)
EMI (Equated Monthly Instalment): The fixed amount paid every month to repay a loan; it covers that month's interest and part of the principal. Early EMIs
are mostly interest.
Example: ₹5,00,000 at 12% a year for 36 months is an EMI of about ₹16,607.

## Tenure
Tenure: The repayment period of a loan, in months or years. A longer tenure lowers the EMI but raises the total interest.
Example: 60 months for a personal loan, 240 months for a home loan.

## Amortisation
Amortisation: Repaying a loan in regular instalments so that the principal reduces to zero by the end of the tenure.
Example: each EMI of a home loan amortises part of the principal.

## Amortisation schedule
Amortisation schedule: A table showing, for every EMI, how much goes to interest, how much to principal, and the balance left.
Example: the schedule shows the balance after the 12th EMI.

## Outstanding principal
Outstanding principal: The part of the principal still unpaid at a point in time.
Example: after 2 years of EMIs, ₹3,60,000 of a ₹5,00,000 loan may still be outstanding.

## Prepayment
Prepayment: Paying back part of the principal before it is due, which reduces the EMI or the tenure.
Example: paying ₹1,00,000 extra towards a home loan after a bonus.

## Part prepayment
Part prepayment: A prepayment of only part of the outstanding principal; the loan continues.
Example: a ₹50,000 part prepayment, then the EMI is recalculated.

## Foreclosure
Foreclosure: Repaying the entire outstanding loan before the end of the tenure and closing the loan account. Also called pre-closure.
Example: closing a 5-year car loan after 3 years.

## Foreclosure charges
Foreclosure charges: A fee some lenders charge when a loan is closed early. **Owner review:** RBI does not allow foreclosure or prepayment
charges on floating-rate term loans taken by individuals for non-business purposes; the amount for other loans is in
the loan agreement.
Example: a fixed-rate personal loan may carry a foreclosure charge stated in the sanction letter.

## Processing fee
Processing fee: A one-time fee for processing a loan application, usually a percentage of the loan amount, often deducted from the
disbursed amount.
Example: a fee stated in the sanction letter and the Key Fact Statement.

## Sanction
Sanction: The lender's approval of a loan for a stated amount, rate and tenure, subject to conditions.
Example: a sanction of ₹25,00,000 at 9% for 20 years.

## Sanction letter
Sanction letter: The written approval of a loan: amount, interest rate, tenure, EMI, fees and conditions to be met before disbursement.
Example: the customer signs and returns the sanction letter to accept the offer.

## Disbursement
Disbursement: Releasing the sanctioned loan money to the borrower or, for a home loan, often to the seller or builder.
Example: a home loan disbursed to the builder in stages.

## Tranche
Tranche: One part of a loan that is disbursed in instalments, such as stage-wise payments for an under-construction house.
Example: the second tranche released when the slab is complete.

## Pre-EMI
Pre-EMI: Interest-only payments on the amount disbursed so far, paid until the full loan is disbursed and full EMIs begin.
Example: pre-EMI paid during construction of a flat.

## Moratorium
Moratorium: A period during which the borrower does not have to pay EMIs (or pays only interest); interest usually still accrues.
Example: an education loan with repayment starting after the course ends.

## Top-up loan
Top-up loan: An additional loan given on top of an existing loan, usually to a borrower with a good repayment record.
Example: a top-up on a home loan for renovation.

## Balance transfer
Balance transfer: Moving an existing loan to another lender, usually for a lower interest rate or better terms; the new lender
pays off the old loan.
Example: transferring a home loan to a lender offering a lower rate.

## Loan agreement
Loan agreement: The legal contract between lender and borrower setting out all terms of the loan.
Example: signed by all borrowers before disbursement.

## Loan account number
Loan account number: The unique number a lender gives a loan; used for payments, statements and closure.
Example: quoted on every EMI receipt.

## Repayment schedule
Repayment schedule: The list of EMI due dates and amounts for the life of the loan.
Example: EMIs due on the 5th of every month.

## Secured loan
Secured loan: A loan backed by collateral that the lender can enforce if the borrower defaults.
Example: a home loan secured by the house.

## Unsecured loan
Unsecured loan: A loan given without collateral, based on the borrower's income and credit record; usually at a higher rate.
Example: a personal loan.

## Collateral
Collateral: An asset pledged or mortgaged as security for a loan.
Example: a property mortgaged for a loan against property.

## Margin money
Margin money: The part of a purchase price the borrower pays from own funds; the loan covers the rest.
Example: on a ₹50,00,000 flat with a ₹40,00,000 loan, the margin is ₹10,00,000.

## Down payment
Down payment: The amount a buyer pays upfront from own funds towards a purchase financed by a loan.
Example: a 20% down payment on a car.

## LTV (Loan to Value)
LTV (Loan to Value): The loan amount as a percentage of the value of the property or asset securing it. A lower LTV means more of the
borrower's own money is in the asset.
Example: a ₹40,00,000 loan on a ₹50,00,000 property is an LTV of 80%.

## FOIR (Fixed Obligation to Income Ratio)
FOIR (Fixed Obligation to Income Ratio): The share of monthly income already committed to fixed payments (existing EMIs, the new EMI and similar). Used to judge
whether a borrower can afford a loan.
Example: EMIs of ₹15,000 on a monthly income of ₹50,000 is a FOIR of 30%.

## DTI (Debt to Income ratio)
DTI (Debt to Income ratio): A measure similar to FOIR: total monthly debt payments divided by monthly income.
Example: ₹20,000 debt payments on ₹60,000 income is a DTI of about 33%.

## Loan eligibility
Loan eligibility: The maximum loan a lender will offer a borrower, based on income, obligations, age, credit record and policy.
Example: eligibility rises when an earning co-applicant is added.

## Ticket size
Ticket size: The loan amount of an individual loan; used to describe typical loan sizes for a product.
Example: small-ticket business loans.

## KFS (Key Fact Statement)
KFS (Key Fact Statement): A standard, simple summary of a loan's key terms (amount, rate, total cost, fees, EMI, recovery and grievance details)
that lenders must give before the loan agreement is signed. **Owner review:** required by RBI for retail and MSME term
loans.
Example: the KFS shows the annual percentage rate and all charges.

## APR (Annual Percentage Rate)
APR (Annual Percentage Rate): The total yearly cost of a loan including interest and fees, expressed as a percentage; shown in the Key Fact Statement.
Example: a 12% interest rate with fees may have an APR of 12.8%.

## MCLR (Marginal Cost of funds based Lending Rate)
MCLR (Marginal Cost of funds based Lending Rate): An internal benchmark rate banks use to price floating-rate loans, based on their own cost of funds; reset periodically.
Example: a loan priced at 1-year MCLR plus a spread.

## Repo rate
Repo rate: The rate at which the Reserve Bank of India lends short-term money to banks. Many floating-rate retail loans are linked to
it, so a repo change can change their EMIs.
Example: a home loan at repo rate plus 2.5%.

## Reverse repo rate
Reverse repo rate: The rate at which RBI borrows money from banks. It is a tool of monetary policy.
Example: announced with the repo rate in RBI's policy.

## EBLR (External Benchmark Lending Rate)
EBLR (External Benchmark Lending Rate): A lending rate linked to an external benchmark such as the repo rate, plus a spread; used for many floating-rate retail
loans.
Example: EBLR = repo rate + lender's spread.

## RLLR (Repo Linked Lending Rate)
RLLR (Repo Linked Lending Rate): A lending rate directly linked to RBI's repo rate.
Example: RLLR moves when RBI changes the repo rate.

## Spread
Spread: The margin a lender adds over a benchmark rate to set the loan's interest rate.
Example: repo rate 6.5% + spread 2.5% = 9%.

## Interest reset
Interest reset: The date on which a floating-rate loan's interest rate is recalculated from its benchmark.
Example: a quarterly reset.

## Base rate
Base rate: An older internal benchmark rate used by banks before MCLR; some old loans are still linked to it.
Example: a loan taken in 2015 linked to base rate.

## Teaser rate
Teaser rate: A low introductory interest rate for the first period of a loan, after which a higher rate applies.
Example: a lower rate for the first two years.

## Credit score
Credit score: A number summarising a person's credit record, used to judge how likely they are to repay. In India scores usually run
from 300 to 900; higher is better.
Example: a score above 750 is generally seen as good.

## CIBIL score
CIBIL score: The credit score produced by TransUnion CIBIL, one of India's credit bureaus; ranges from 300 to 900.
Example: "CIBIL 780" in a credit report.

## Credit bureau
Credit bureau: A credit information company that collects repayment records from lenders and produces credit reports and scores. India
has four: TransUnion CIBIL, Experian, Equifax and CRIF High Mark.
Example: a lender pulls a report from a bureau before sanction.

## Credit report
Credit report: The bureau's record of a person's loans and cards: accounts, limits, balances, repayment history, defaults and enquiries.
Example: the report shows two active loans and no overdue.

## Credit history
Credit history: How a person has handled loans and credit cards over time.
Example: 5 years of on-time EMIs is a strong credit history.

## Credit utilisation
Credit utilisation: The share of available credit-card limits being used; high utilisation can lower a credit score.
Example: ₹60,000 used on a ₹1,00,000 limit is 60% utilisation.

## Hard enquiry
Hard enquiry: A credit-report check made by a lender when someone applies for credit; many in a short time can lower the score.
Example: applying to five lenders in a month creates five hard enquiries.

## Soft enquiry
Soft enquiry: A check of one's own credit report, or a pre-approval check, that does not affect the score.
Example: checking your own CIBIL report online.

## DPD (Days Past Due)
DPD (Days Past Due): How many days a payment is overdue; credit reports show DPD month by month.
Example: "30 DPD" means the EMI is 30 days late.

## SMA (Special Mention Account)
SMA (Special Mention Account): RBI's classification of loans showing early stress: SMA-0 (overdue 1 to 30 days), SMA-1 (31 to 60), SMA-2 (61 to 90).
Example: an account 45 days overdue is SMA-1.

## NPA (Non-Performing Asset)
NPA (Non-Performing Asset): A loan whose principal or interest has been overdue for more than 90 days.
Example: an account unpaid for 4 months becomes an NPA.

## Write-off
Write-off: Removing a loan from the lender's books as unrecoverable; the borrower still owes the money and it shows on the credit
report.
Example: a credit report showing "written off" hurts future borrowing.

## Settlement
Settlement: An agreement where the lender accepts less than the full dues to close a loan; reported as "settled", which hurts the
credit score.
Example: a loan "settled" for 60% of the dues.

## Wilful defaulter
Wilful defaulter: A borrower who can pay but deliberately does not, or diverts funds; lenders report and restrict such borrowers.
Example: a defaulter identified under RBI's wilful-defaulter rules.

## Credit appraisal
Credit appraisal: The lender's assessment of a loan application: income, obligations, credit record, documents and the security.
Example: done by the credit team after the file reaches them.

## Underwriting
Underwriting: Deciding whether to lend, how much and on what terms, after appraisal.
Example: an underwriter approves the loan at a lower amount.

## Credit memo
Credit memo: The note in which a credit officer records the analysis and recommendation for a loan.
Example: the memo lists strengths, risks and conditions.

## Deviation
Deviation: An approved exception to a credit policy rule, such as a higher FOIR than the policy allows, approved by an authority.
Example: a deviation for age approved by the credit head.

## Risk grade
Risk grade: A rating a lender gives a borrower or loan to show its expected risk.
Example: grade A for a salaried borrower with a high score.

## Default
Default: Failure to pay EMIs or meet loan terms as agreed.
Example: missing three EMIs in a row.

## Delinquency
Delinquency: A loan that is overdue; the start of possible default.
Example: delinquency at 30 DPD.

## Recovery
Recovery: Collecting overdue dues from a defaulted borrower, by legal or other permitted means.
Example: recovery through the courts or enforcement of security.

## SARFAESI Act
SARFAESI Act: The Securitisation and Reconstruction of Financial Assets and Enforcement of Security Interest Act, 2002; it lets secured
lenders enforce security (such as taking possession of a mortgaged property) without going to court, under set rules.
Example: a notice under the Act after a home loan becomes an NPA.

## CERSAI
CERSAI: Central Registry of Securitisation Asset Reconstruction and Security Interest of India: where lenders register charges on
assets given as security, so the same asset cannot be secretly pledged twice. It also hosts the Central KYC registry.
Example: a home loan's mortgage registered with CERSAI.

## Charge
Charge: A lender's legal right over an asset given as security for a loan.
Example: a charge created on a property for a loan against property.

## Lien
Lien: The right to hold an asset until a debt is paid.
Example: a lien marked on a fixed deposit.

## Mortgage
Mortgage: Giving an interest in an immovable property as security for a loan.
Example: a house mortgaged for a home loan.

## Equitable mortgage
Equitable mortgage: A mortgage created by depositing the original title deeds with the lender, with intent to create security; common for home
loans.
Example: the borrower deposits the sale deed with the lender.

## Registered mortgage
Registered mortgage: A mortgage created by a registered mortgage deed at the sub-registrar's office.
Example: required in some states or for some loans.

## Hypothecation
Hypothecation: Security over movable assets that stay with the borrower, such as a vehicle; the lender can take them on default.
Example: a car loan with the car hypothecated to the lender.

## Pledge
Pledge: Giving possession of a movable asset to the lender as security.
Example: gold pledged for a gold loan.

## Guarantee
Guarantee: A promise by a third person to repay the loan if the borrower does not.
Example: a guarantee signed by a relative.

## Guarantor
Guarantor: The person who gives a guarantee. Not a borrower; becomes liable only if the borrower defaults.
Example: a parent standing guarantor for a son's loan.

## Co-applicant
Co-applicant: A person who applies for the loan together with the primary applicant; their income can count for eligibility and they are
equally liable to repay.
Example: a spouse added as co-applicant to a home loan.

## Co-borrower
Co-borrower: Another term for a co-applicant who shares the liability to repay.
Example: both spouses are co-borrowers on the loan.

## Primary applicant
Primary applicant: The main borrower in whose name the loan is applied for.
Example: the salaried husband as primary applicant, wife as co-applicant.

## Borrower
Borrower: The person or entity that takes the loan and must repay it.
Example: the borrower signs the loan agreement.

## Joint loan
Joint loan: A loan taken by two or more borrowers who are jointly responsible for repayment.
Example: a joint home loan for a couple who co-own the house.

## KYC (Know Your Customer)
KYC (Know Your Customer): The process of verifying a customer's identity and address with valid documents before giving a loan.
Example: PAN and Aadhaar collected and verified at FOS.

## CKYC (Central KYC)
CKYC (Central KYC): India's central registry of KYC records (CKYCR), run by CERSAI; a customer's KYC is stored once and reused by regulated
lenders through a KYC identifier.
Example: a lender fetches a customer's record from CKYCR.

## KIN (KYC Identification Number)
KIN (KYC Identification Number): The 14-digit number the Central KYC registry gives a customer's KYC record.
Example: quoting the KIN lets a lender fetch the record.

## e-KYC
e-KYC: KYC done electronically, for example through Aadhaar OTP authentication.
Example: Aadhaar OTP e-KYC during online onboarding.

## Offline Aadhaar verification
Offline Aadhaar verification: Verifying identity from a digitally signed Aadhaar XML or QR code without sharing the full Aadhaar number.
Example: the customer shares the Aadhaar XML file.

## Masked Aadhaar
Masked Aadhaar: An Aadhaar copy showing only the last four digits of the number.
Example: lenders store masked Aadhaar to protect the number.

## V-CIP (Video-based Customer Identification Process)
V-CIP (Video-based Customer Identification Process): KYC done on a live video call with the lender's official, with consent, where the customer shows original documents.
Example: completing KYC without visiting a branch.

## Video KYC
Video KYC: Common name for V-CIP.
Example: a video KYC call scheduled for the customer.

## Re-KYC
Re-KYC: Updating a customer's KYC periodically or when details change.
Example: a re-KYC request after an address change.

## PAN (Permanent Account Number)
PAN (Permanent Account Number): The 10-character tax identification number issued by the Income Tax Department; needed for most loans.
Example: five letters, four digits and a final letter, printed on the PAN card.

## Form 60
Form 60: A declaration filed by a person who does not have a PAN, in place of PAN for certain transactions.
Example: used when a customer has applied for PAN but not received it.

## PEP (Politically Exposed Person)
PEP (Politically Exposed Person): A person holding or who held a prominent public function; lenders apply enhanced checks to such customers.
Example: a senior government official.

## AML (Anti-Money Laundering)
AML (Anti-Money Laundering): Rules and checks to prevent loans and accounts being used to launder money.
Example: screening customers against sanctions lists.

## CDD (Customer Due Diligence)
CDD (Customer Due Diligence): Identifying and verifying a customer and understanding the purpose of the relationship.
Example: KYC plus checking the source of income.

## EDD (Enhanced Due Diligence)
EDD (Enhanced Due Diligence): Extra checks for higher-risk customers.
Example: EDD for a PEP.

## Beneficial owner
Beneficial owner: The person who ultimately owns or controls a company or entity that borrows.
Example: the majority shareholder of a borrowing company.

## Liveness check
Liveness check: A check that a live person, not a photo or video, is present during digital KYC.
Example: asking the customer to blink on camera.

## Face match
Face match: Comparing the customer's live photo with the photo on an ID document.
Example: face match during video KYC.

## e-Sign
e-Sign: A legally valid electronic signature, for example Aadhaar-based e-Sign.
Example: the loan agreement e-signed by the borrower.

## DigiLocker
DigiLocker: The government's digital document wallet; documents fetched from it are treated as verified copies.
Example: driving licence fetched from DigiLocker.

## Account Aggregator
Account Aggregator: An RBI-regulated system that lets a customer share bank and financial data digitally with a lender, with consent.
Example: bank statements shared through an Account Aggregator.

## Identity proof
Identity proof: A document that proves who the customer is.
Example: PAN card, passport, voter ID.

## Address proof
Address proof: A document that proves where the customer lives.
Example: passport, driving licence or voter ID with the current address.

## Income proof
Income proof: Documents that show the customer's income.
Example: salary slips, Form 16 or ITR.

## Salary slip
Salary slip: A monthly statement from the employer showing gross pay, deductions and net (take-home) pay.
Example: the last three months' salary slips.

## Gross salary
Gross salary: Pay before deductions such as tax and provident fund.
Example: gross ₹60,000, net ₹50,000.

## Net salary
Net salary: Take-home pay after deductions.
Example: ₹50,000 credited to the bank account.

## Form 16
Form 16: The yearly certificate an employer gives showing salary paid and tax (TDS) deducted.
Example: Form 16 for the last financial year.

## ITR (Income Tax Return)
ITR (Income Tax Return): The yearly statement of income filed with the Income Tax Department.
Example: the last two years' ITRs for a self-employed borrower.

## ITR-V
ITR-V: The acknowledgement of an income tax return filed online.
Example: ITR-V showing the filing date.

## Computation of income
Computation of income: A statement, usually prepared with the ITR, showing how taxable income was calculated.
Example: submitted with the ITR by a self-employed applicant.

## Form 26AS
Form 26AS: A statement from the Income Tax Department showing tax deducted and paid against a PAN.
Example: used to cross-check declared income.

## AIS (Annual Information Statement)
AIS (Annual Information Statement): A detailed income-tax statement of a taxpayer's financial transactions and income for a year.
Example: checked along with Form 26AS.

## GST return
GST return: A periodic return a GST-registered business files with sales and tax details.
Example: GSTR-3B used to check a business's turnover.

## GSTR-3B
GSTR-3B: The monthly or quarterly GST summary return of sales, purchases and tax paid.
Example: last 12 months' GSTR-3B for a business loan.

## Bank statement
Bank statement: A record of all transactions in a bank account over a period; used to check income, obligations and banking habits.
Example: the last 6 months' statement of the salary account.

## Balance sheet
Balance sheet: A statement of a business's assets, liabilities and capital on a date.
Example: audited balance sheet for the last year.

## Profit and loss statement
Profit and loss statement: A statement of a business's income, expenses and profit for a period.
Example: P&L for the last two years.

## Audited financials
Audited financials: Financial statements checked and certified by a chartered accountant.
Example: required for larger business loans.

## Udyam registration
Udyam registration: The government registration of a micro, small or medium enterprise (MSME), with an Udyam number.
Example: Udyam certificate as business proof.

## Business proof
Business proof: Documents showing a business exists and has been running.
Example: GST certificate, Udyam certificate, shop and establishment licence.

## Shop and establishment licence
Shop and establishment licence: A state licence for shops and commercial establishments; often used as business proof.
Example: issued by the municipal authority.

## Employment proof
Employment proof: Documents showing a salaried person's job.
Example: appointment letter or employee ID.

## Property documents
Property documents: Papers that prove ownership and status of a property offered as security.
Example: sale deed, chain documents, encumbrance certificate.

## Sale deed
Sale deed: The registered document that transfers ownership of a property from seller to buyer.
Example: the main title document for a resale house.

## Agreement for sale
Agreement for sale: An agreement in which the seller agrees to sell and the buyer to buy a property on stated terms, before the sale deed.
Example: signed with the builder for an under-construction flat.

## Title deed
Title deed: A document that proves a person's ownership of a property.
Example: the sale deed in the owner's name.

## Chain documents
Chain documents: The series of earlier title documents showing how ownership passed to the current owner.
Example: previous sale deeds for the last 13 to 30 years.

## EC (Encumbrance Certificate)
EC (Encumbrance Certificate): A certificate from the sub-registrar listing registered transactions on a property, showing whether it carries a loan or
other charge.
Example: an EC for the last 13 years.

## Property tax receipt
Property tax receipt: Proof that the property tax has been paid; also shows the owner and the property's municipal details.
Example: latest property tax receipt.

## OC (Occupancy Certificate)
OC (Occupancy Certificate): A certificate from the local authority that a building is fit to occupy.
Example: required before moving into a new flat.

## Completion certificate
Completion certificate: A certificate that a building has been completed as per the approved plan.
Example: issued by the municipal authority.

## Approved building plan
Approved building plan: The building plan sanctioned by the local authority.
Example: checked during technical and legal verification.

## NOC (No Objection Certificate)
NOC (No Objection Certificate): A letter saying the issuer has no objection to a transaction, such as a society's NOC to mortgage a flat, or a lender's
NOC after a loan is closed.
Example: builder's NOC for a home loan on an allotted flat.

## Allotment letter
Allotment letter: A builder's or authority's letter allotting a property to a buyer.
Example: allotment letter for a flat in a new project.

## Tripartite agreement
Tripartite agreement: An agreement between the buyer, the builder and the lender for a loan on an under-construction property.
Example: signed for a home loan on a new project.

## Power of attorney
Power of attorney: A legal document authorising a person to act on behalf of another.
Example: an NRI borrower's POA holder signing documents.

## Legal opinion
Legal opinion: A lawyer's report on whether a property's title is clear and can be taken as security.
Example: a title search report from the lender's advocate.

## Valuation report
Valuation report: A valuer's report estimating a property's market value and condition.
Example: used to calculate the LTV.

## Cancelled cheque
Cancelled cheque: A cheque with "cancelled" written across it, given to share bank account details.
Example: for setting up the EMI mandate.

## PDC (Post-Dated Cheque)
PDC (Post-Dated Cheque): A cheque dated in the future, given in advance for EMIs or as security.
Example: security PDCs collected at disbursement.

## Utility bill
Utility bill: A bill for electricity, water, gas or phone; sometimes accepted as address proof for a limited time.
Example: an electricity bill not older than two months.

## Rent agreement
Rent agreement: A contract between a landlord and a tenant; may show a tenant's address.
Example: a registered rent agreement.

## Passport
Passport: The government travel document; accepted as identity and address proof.
Example: passport with the current address.

## Voter ID
Voter ID: The Election Commission's identity card (EPIC); accepted as identity and address proof.
Example: voter ID card.

## Driving licence
Driving licence: The licence to drive issued by the transport authority; accepted as identity and address proof.
Example: driving licence with the current address.

## Aadhaar
Aadhaar: The 12-digit identity number issued by UIDAI, linked to biometrics; used for identity and address under the KYC rules.
Example: masked Aadhaar collected for address.

## NREGA job card
NREGA job card: The job card issued under the rural employment guarantee scheme; an officially valid document for KYC.
Example: used by rural applicants.

## Photograph
Photograph: A recent passport-size photo of each applicant, collected with the application.
Example: one photo per applicant and co-applicant.

## Signature verification
Signature verification: Checking that a person's signature matches across documents and the application.
Example: signature on the form matched with the PAN card.

## Lead
Lead: A potential customer interested in a loan.
Example: a walk-in enquiry at a branch.

## Sourcing
Sourcing: Finding and bringing in loan applications, by the lender's staff or partners.
Example: an FOS officer sourcing home-loan leads.

## File login
File login: Formally submitting a loan application with documents into the lender's system.
Example: the case is logged in once the form and KYC are complete.

## Application form
Application form: The form in which the applicant gives personal, income and loan details.
Example: filled at the first visit.

## Field investigation
Field investigation: A visit by the lender or its agency to the customer's home or office to verify details. Also called FI.
Example: residence FI before sanction.

## Residence verification
Residence verification: A visit to confirm that the customer lives at the stated address.
Example: neighbours confirm the customer lives there.

## Office verification
Office verification: A visit to confirm the customer's workplace or business.
Example: verifying a shop's existence.

## TVR (Telephone Verification)
TVR (Telephone Verification): A phone call to verify details given in the application.
Example: calling the employer's HR.

## PD (Personal Discussion)
PD (Personal Discussion): An interview of the customer by the lender to understand income, business, obligations and the loan's purpose.
Example: PD at the business premises.

## Legal verification
Legal verification: A lawyer's check of a property's title documents.
Example: confirming clear title before disbursement.

## Technical verification
Technical verification: An engineer's or valuer's inspection of a property: construction, approvals and market value.
Example: technical visit to the flat.

## RCU (Risk Containment Unit)
RCU (Risk Containment Unit): A unit that samples and checks files and documents for fraud or misrepresentation.
Example: RCU screens the income documents.

## Dedupe
Dedupe: Checking whether the applicant already exists in the lender's records or appears in negative lists.
Example: dedupe finds an existing loan with the same lender.

## Fraud check
Fraud check: Checks for forged documents, identity fraud or misrepresentation.
Example: document sampling by RCU.

## PDD (Post-Disbursal Documents)
PDD (Post-Disbursal Documents): Documents to be collected after disbursement, as agreed in the sanction.
Example: registered sale deed collected after registration.

## Loan booking
Loan booking: Creating the loan account in the lender's system at disbursement.
Example: the loan is booked and the account number generated.

## Welcome kit
Welcome kit: The documents a borrower receives after disbursement: loan details, schedule and service contacts.
Example: sent by email after the loan is booked.

## FOS (Field Officer Sales)
FOS (Field Officer Sales): The first stage in this system, where the field officer collects the customer's details and documents.
Example: a case starts at FOS.

## CPA
CPA: The stage after FOS in this system, where the completed file is processed further. The full form is not configured here.
Example: a case moves from FOS to CPA when every FOS check passes.

## BOPS
BOPS: A later stage of this system's loan process, after RCU. The full form is not configured here.
Example: FOS, CPA, Credit, RCU, BOPS, HOPS, Disbursement.

## HOPS
HOPS: A later stage of this system's loan process, after BOPS and before disbursement. The full form is not configured here.
Example: the stage before disbursement.

## DSA (Direct Selling Agent)
DSA (Direct Selling Agent): An agent or firm that sources loan applications for a lender, for a commission.
Example: a DSA bringing home-loan cases.

## Connector
Connector: A person who refers potential borrowers to a lender or DSA.
Example: a local referral partner.

## Channel partner
Channel partner: A business partner that sources or services loans for a lender.
Example: a builder tie-up.

## Builder
Builder: The developer constructing a project; often a party in home loans for under-construction property.
Example: payments released to the builder in stages.

## Valuer
Valuer: An empanelled professional who values a property for the lender.
Example: the valuer's report gives the market value.

## Panel advocate
Panel advocate: A lawyer empanelled by the lender to give legal opinions on property titles.
Example: the advocate's title search report.

## Collection agent
Collection agent: A person authorised by the lender to collect overdue dues, under the lender's code of conduct.
Example: must follow RBI's fair practices on collection.

## Grievance redressal officer
Grievance redressal officer: The officer a lender appoints to handle customer complaints.
Example: named in the KFS and on the lender's website.

## Ombudsman
Ombudsman: RBI's Integrated Ombudsman, to whom a customer can complain if a regulated lender does not resolve a complaint.
Example: complaint filed after 30 days without a reply.

## NACH (National Automated Clearing House)
NACH (National Automated Clearing House): NPCI's system for automatic EMI debits from a borrower's bank account, under a mandate the borrower signs.
Example: EMI collected through NACH on the due date.

## e-NACH
e-NACH: A NACH mandate registered digitally, through net banking, debit card or Aadhaar.
Example: e-NACH set up during onboarding.

## e-mandate
e-mandate: A digital authorisation for recurring debits, such as e-NACH or UPI AutoPay.
Example: EMI e-mandate on the salary account.

## ECS (Electronic Clearing Service)
ECS (Electronic Clearing Service): The older system for automatic debits, largely replaced by NACH.
Example: older loans with ECS mandates.

## Standing instruction
Standing instruction: An instruction to a bank to make a recurring payment from an account.
Example: SI for EMIs from an account with the same bank.

## UPI AutoPay
UPI AutoPay: Recurring payments set up through UPI with the customer's consent.
Example: small EMIs collected through UPI AutoPay.

## Bounce
Bounce: A failed payment because of insufficient balance, a stopped mandate or a technical reason.
Example: an EMI bounce reported in the statement.

## Bounce charges
Bounce charges: A charge a lender applies when an EMI payment bounces; the amount is stated in the loan terms.
Example: a charge listed in the KFS.

## Penal charges
Penal charges: Charges for non-compliance such as late payment. **Owner review:** RBI requires these as fixed "penal charges", not added
to the interest rate.
Example: penal charges for a delayed EMI.

## Overdue
Overdue: An amount past its due date and unpaid.
Example: two EMIs overdue.

## Due date
Due date: The date by which an EMI must be paid.
Example: the 5th of each month.

## Grace period
Grace period: Extra days after a due date before a payment counts as late, if the loan terms allow it.
Example: a 3-day grace period.

## SOA (Statement of Account)
SOA (Statement of Account): A statement of a loan's transactions: disbursements, EMIs, charges and balance.
Example: downloaded from the lender's portal.

## Foreclosure letter
Foreclosure letter: A letter from the lender stating the exact amount needed to close the loan on a date.
Example: requested before closing a loan.

## No dues certificate
No dues certificate: A letter confirming a loan has been fully repaid and nothing is owed.
Example: issued after the last EMI.

## Release of property documents
Release of property documents: Returning the original property documents to the borrower after a secured loan is closed. **Owner review:** RBI requires
this within 30 days of closure, with compensation for delay.
Example: original sale deed returned after closure.

## Home loan
Home loan: A loan to buy, build or renovate a residential property, secured by that property.
Example: a 20-year home loan for a flat.

## Home improvement loan
Home improvement loan: A loan for repairs or renovation of an existing home.
Example: a loan for a new kitchen and painting.

## Home extension loan
Home extension loan: A loan to add space to an existing home.
Example: adding a floor.

## Plot loan
Plot loan: A loan to buy a residential plot of land.
Example: a plot in an approved layout.

## Composite loan
Composite loan: A loan to buy a plot and build a house on it.
Example: plot purchase and construction in one loan.

## LAP (Loan Against Property)
LAP (Loan Against Property): A secured loan against a residential or commercial property the borrower owns, for any legitimate purpose.
Example: LAP for business expansion.

## Personal loan
Personal loan: An unsecured loan for personal needs, given on income and credit record.
Example: a 3-year personal loan for a wedding.

## Business loan
Business loan: A loan for a business's needs, secured or unsecured.
Example: an unsecured business loan for inventory.

## MSME loan
MSME loan: A loan to a micro, small or medium enterprise.
Example: working capital for a small manufacturer.

## Working capital
Working capital: Money a business needs for day-to-day operations: stock, receivables, wages.
Example: a cash-credit limit for working capital.

## Overdraft
Overdraft: A facility to withdraw more than the account balance up to a limit; interest only on the amount used.
Example: an overdraft against a property.

## Cash credit
Cash credit: A revolving working-capital limit, usually against stock and receivables.
Example: a cash-credit account for a trader.

## Term loan
Term loan: A loan repaid over a fixed period in instalments.
Example: a 5-year term loan for machinery.

## Gold loan
Gold loan: A loan secured by pledged gold jewellery.
Example: a loan against 50 grams of gold.

## Vehicle loan
Vehicle loan: A loan to buy a car, two-wheeler or commercial vehicle, with the vehicle hypothecated.
Example: a car loan over 5 years.

## Education loan
Education loan: A loan for higher studies, often with a moratorium until the course ends.
Example: a loan for a master's degree.

## Loan against securities
Loan against securities: A loan against shares, mutual funds or bonds pledged with the lender.
Example: an overdraft against mutual funds.

## NBFC (Non-Banking Financial Company)
NBFC (Non-Banking Financial Company): A company registered with RBI that lends but does not hold a banking licence.
Example: an NBFC giving loans against property.

## HFC (Housing Finance Company)
HFC (Housing Finance Company): An NBFC whose main business is housing loans.
Example: a housing finance company giving home loans.

## PMAY (Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana)
PMAY (Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana): The government's housing scheme for affordable housing, including interest-subsidy schemes for eligible buyers.
**Owner review:** the scheme's eligibility and subsidy rules change; check the current version.
Example: an eligible first-time buyer applying for a subsidy.

## Affordable housing
Affordable housing: Lower-cost housing for low- and middle-income buyers, often with policy support.
Example: a small flat under an affordable-housing scheme.

## Section 80C
Section 80C: The income-tax section allowing a deduction, within its overall limit, for home-loan principal repayment among other
items. **Owner review:** applies under the old tax regime; limits can change.
Example: principal repaid counts towards the 80C limit.

## Section 24(b)
Section 24(b): The income-tax section allowing a deduction for home-loan interest. **Owner review:** limits and the regime that allows it
can change.
Example: interest on a self-occupied home loan claimed under 24(b).

## Net monthly income
Net monthly income: Income after deductions, used for eligibility.
Example: net salary credited each month.

## Fixed obligations
Fixed obligations: Regular monthly payments a borrower already has, such as other EMIs.
Example: a car EMI of ₹8,000.

## Disposable income
Disposable income: Income left after taxes and fixed obligations.
Example: ₹50,000 net minus ₹15,000 EMIs leaves ₹35,000.

## Banking surplus
Banking surplus: The money regularly left in a business's bank account, used to assess repayment capacity.
Example: monthly credits minus debits.

## ABB (Average Bank Balance)
ABB (Average Bank Balance): The average balance in an account over a period, used to assess a borrower's repayment capacity.
Example: ABB of the last 6 months.

## Turnover
Turnover: A business's total sales over a period.
Example: ₹1 crore annual turnover from GST returns.

## Business vintage
Business vintage: How long a business has been operating.
Example: 3 years of business vintage.

## Employment stability
Employment stability: How long a salaried person has worked with the current employer and overall.
Example: 2 years with the current employer.

## Age at maturity
Age at maturity: The borrower's age when the loan ends; lenders limit it in their policy.
Example: a 20-year loan taken at 45 ends at 65.

## Income clubbing
Income clubbing: Adding a co-applicant's income to the primary applicant's for eligibility.
Example: spouse's salary clubbed to raise eligibility.

## RBI (Reserve Bank of India)
RBI (Reserve Bank of India): India's central bank and the regulator of banks and NBFCs.
Example: RBI sets the repo rate.

## NHB (National Housing Bank)
NHB (National Housing Bank): A regulator and refinancer for housing finance companies.
Example: refinance to HFCs.

## Fair Practices Code
Fair Practices Code: Rules lenders follow on transparency, fair treatment, recovery and grievance handling.
Example: telling the borrower reasons for rejecting a loan.

## Digital lending
Digital lending: Lending through digital channels and apps. **Owner review:** governed by RBI's digital lending rules (disclosures,
consent, direct disbursal to the borrower's account).
Example: a loan applied and approved in an app.

## Cooling-off period
Cooling-off period: A period after taking a digital loan in which the borrower can exit by repaying principal and proportionate interest.
**Owner review:** defined in RBI's digital lending rules.
Example: exiting within the look-up period without penalty.

## Data consent
Data consent: The customer's permission to collect and use their data for a stated purpose.
Example: consent for a credit-bureau check.

## Loan to income
Loan to income: The loan amount compared with yearly income; used as a rough affordability check.
Example: a ₹30,00,000 loan on ₹6,00,000 yearly income is 5 times income.
